# AI Portfolio Intelligence - Master Project Plan

Status: planning baseline; Phase 1 implemented
Research checked: August 15, 2026
Working title only; choose a product name later.

> Implementation update, August 15, 2026: the active pilot is Telegram-only. The responsive
> web application is deferred until the Telegram workflow proves that users repeatedly value
> the brief, risk alerts, and ticker reviews. See
> [`phases/phase-01-telegram-agent`](../phases/phase-01-telegram-agent/) for the running MVP.

## 1. The product in one sentence

Build a cross-broker, read-only portfolio copilot that researches the market once, maps material changes to each user's actual holdings and risk policy, and produces evidence-backed decision cards with clear risks, timestamps, and invalidation conditions.

## 2. Honest verdict

The project is technically feasible and useful, but the broad idea is no longer new. In 2026:

- PortfolioPilot connects portfolios and gives personalized portfolio guidance.
- Robinhood offers Strategies, Cortex, and an official Agentic Trading MCP.
- Public offers portfolio-aware research and user-defined agents that can monitor conditions and execute approved rules.
- Composer automates rule-based portfolio strategies.

This means the project should not be positioned as "an AI that reads news and tells me what to buy." That is crowded and difficult to trust.

The initial wedge should be:

> An independent, cross-broker portfolio watchtower for concentrated DIY investors: transparent evidence, portfolio-policy checks, change detection, risk-first alerts, and a replayable decision history.

The product can be an excellent private tool and a strong engineering project. A public, paid advisory business is possible only after a legal and regulatory plan is chosen.

## 4. What is in the MVP

An invited user can:

1. Register and sign in.
2. Complete a structured investment-policy questionnaire.
3. Add cash and holdings manually or import a standard CSV.
4. See portfolio value, allocation, gains/losses, sector exposure, concentration, volatility, drawdown, and upcoming earnings.
5. Receive a morning portfolio brief based on one shared market snapshot.
6. See material event alerts for owned or watched securities.
7. Open a decision card explaining why an item matters to this portfolio.
8. Inspect every supporting source and its timestamp.
9. Ask grounded questions about the portfolio.
10. Record whether a decision card was useful, ignored, or simulated in a paper portfolio.

### Decision-card states

- HOLD / NO MATERIAL CHANGE
- WATCH
- REVIEW FOR ADDITION
- REVIEW FOR REDUCTION
- HIGH EVENT RISK
- POLICY VIOLATION
- INSUFFICIENT DATA

For the private paper-trading mode, a card may include a simulated allocation range. The live product must not issue personalized buy/sell instructions until the regulatory path is approved.

### Every decision card must contain

- Ticker and security name.
- Why the item was generated now.
- Current position value and portfolio weight.
- Portfolio-policy checks that passed or failed.
- Bull case and bear case.
- Material catalysts and dates.
- Principal risks.
- Relevant deterministic metrics.
- Confidence and data-quality status.
- Source links and source timestamps.
- Conditions that invalidate the conclusion.
- Difference from the prior decision card.
- A clear indication that this is live analysis or paper simulation.

## 5. Explicit MVP exclusions

Do not implement any of these in the first build:

- Automatic live trading.
- Options, margin, short selling, leveraged or inverse products.
- High-frequency or day-trading strategies.
- Guaranteed returns or price targets generated by an LLM.
- Tax filing or definitive tax advice.
- Bank budgeting, payroll, bill payment, or credit-card connections.
- 401(k) contribution optimization.
- Crypto trading.
- Social/copy trading.
- A native Android application.
- A full scan of every U.S. security every two hours.

## 6. Core architectural idea

```text
Data providers, SEC EDGAR, FRED, licensed news
                       |
                       v
        Shared ingestion and normalization
                       |
                       v
    Shared market/security intelligence store
       prices | events | features | evidence
                       |
            material-change events
                       |
       +---------------+---------------+
       |               |               |
       v               v               v
   User A policy   User B policy   User C policy
   + portfolio     + portfolio     + portfolio
       |               |               |
       v               v               v
   deterministic compatibility and risk checks
                       |
                       v
       grounded explanation and decision card
```

The expensive work happens once per security or event. Personalization is a small calculation over the relevant user's portfolio and policy.

## 7. Research scope and cadence

Do not ask an LLM to "deep research the entire market" every two hours. It is costly, noisy, hard to audit, and unnecessary.

Use a tiered universe:

### Tier A - continuously relevant

- All current user holdings.
- All user watchlists.
- SPY, QQQ, IWM, major sector ETFs, Treasury-rate and volatility indicators.
- Securities already present in an active decision card.

Refresh structured prices frequently enough for the licensed feed. Check news and filing events every 15-30 minutes or consume provider webhooks/streams when available.

### Tier B - bounded opportunity universe

- Start with liquid U.S. large- and mid-cap equities or a defined index membership list.
- Run structured screens after market close.
- Promote only the small top-ranked set into deeper document research.

### Tier C - on demand

- A ticker requested by a user.
- A security referenced by a material event.

### Scheduled workflows

| Time | Work |
|---|---|
| Pre-market | Market regime, overnight news, earnings/filing changes, portfolio-impact brief |
| During market | Prices for Tier A, material events, stale-data checks; personalize only on meaningful change |
| Post-market | Recompute returns/risk, update fundamentals/events, run bounded universe screen |
| Weekly | Sector and macro review, factor diagnostics, model drift report |
| On event | Reprocess only affected securities, then only affected users |

Every job must be idempotent, retryable, observable, and safe to replay.

## 8. Data-source strategy

Create interfaces so providers can be replaced without changing business logic:

- `MarketDataProvider`: quotes, bars, corporate actions, symbols, calendars.
- `FundamentalsProvider`: statements, ratios, estimates when licensed.
- `NewsProvider`: articles, metadata, entitlements, canonical URLs.
- `FilingProvider`: SEC submissions, filings, XBRL facts.
- `MacroProvider`: FRED/ALFRED series and release vintages.
- `BrokerProvider`: accounts, balances, positions, transactions, connection health.

Recommended baseline:

- SEC EDGAR for U.S. filings and XBRL facts.
- FRED for current macro data and ALFRED vintages for honest backtests.
- One commercial U.S. equities provider chosen only after confirming business display, derived-data, storage, and redistribution rights.
- Manual/CSV portfolio data initially.

Free or personal-use market-data plans must not be assumed to cover a five-user or commercial app. Provider licensing is an explicit launch gate.

## 9. Brokerage integration plan

### Phase 1: manual and CSV

This is the reliable MVP path. Define a canonical CSV template and generate an import report listing accepted, rejected, and ambiguous rows.

### Phase 2: read-only aggregation

Evaluate:

- SnapTrade when multi-broker investing data and a future trading path matter.
- Plaid Investments when read-only holdings and transactions are sufficient and daily/refresh-based data is acceptable.

Never store brokerage passwords. Use the provider-hosted authorization flow and encrypt provider tokens.

### Robinhood-specific 2026 finding

Robinhood now provides an official Trading MCP. Its support documentation says an authorized agent can read all Robinhood accounts, positions, balances, transactions, watchlists, and scans. Order placement is restricted to a dedicated Robinhood Agentic account.

However, Robinhood's customer agreement says third parties may not use the API package or develop licensee products without Robinhood's express written consent. Therefore:

- Personal/internal technical experiment: evaluate the MCP in an isolated, small-capital Agentic account.
- Multi-user product: obtain written commercial approval or use an approved aggregator.
- MVP: do not make Robinhood connectivity a dependency.
- Never use unofficial Robinhood endpoints or browser automation.

## 10. Recommendation engine

The recommendation system is a pipeline, not one prompt.

### Stage 1 - data validation

- Reject missing, contradictory, improperly adjusted, or stale data.
- Normalize symbols, timestamps, currencies, splits, and source identifiers.
- De-duplicate news and link each item to the correct security.
- Preserve raw source material and ingestion time.

### Stage 2 - deterministic feature calculation

Calculate in code:

- Return and drawdown windows.
- Volatility and liquidity.
- Position and sector concentration.
- Correlation and duplicate exposure.
- Valuation/fundamental features.
- Earnings and filing event proximity.
- Unrealized gain/loss and holding period when tax lots exist.
- Cash reserve and maximum allocation constraints.

### Stage 3 - candidate generation

Use configurable rules and a versioned scoring model. Do not tune weights on future data. A candidate must meet minimum liquidity, freshness, evidence, and investability requirements before personalization.

### Stage 4 - user compatibility

Apply hard constraints before ranking:

- Maximum position and sector weight.
- Minimum cash reserve.
- Allowed security types.
- Exclusions.
- Risk tolerance and horizon.
- Account type and tax sensitivity.
- Maximum acceptable drawdown.
- Data freshness and minimum confidence.

A hard-policy failure can never be overridden by the LLM.

### Stage 5 - paper allocation

Use a deterministic optimizer or transparent rule set. Validate that weights sum correctly and remain inside all constraints. Include transaction-cost, slippage, turnover, and tax estimates where data exists.

### Stage 6 - grounded explanation

Give Bedrock only:

- Validated metrics.
- The relevant portfolio-policy results.
- A small evidence set.
- A strict JSON response schema.

The model explains the decision and its counter-case. It cannot invent a ticker, alter a calculated number, loosen a risk limit, or call a trading tool.

### Stage 7 - post-generation validation

- Validate JSON schema.
- Verify every factual claim maps to an evidence ID or calculation ID.
- Recheck numbers against authoritative structured values.
- Block unsupported superlatives and guarantees.
- Return `INSUFFICIENT_DATA` on failure.

## 11. Structured investment policy

Store these as fields, not just chat text:

- Risk tolerance and maximum drawdown tolerance.
- Goal and time horizon.
- Minimum cash reserve.
- Maximum individual-position weight.
- Maximum sector weight.
- Allowed and excluded asset types.
- Excluded companies/sectors.
- ETF versus individual-stock preference.
- Income/dividend preference.
- Tax sensitivity.
- Short-term activity allowed or disallowed.
- Account type: taxable, traditional IRA, Roth IRA, 401(k), other.
- Fractional-share support.
- Minimum evidence/confidence threshold.
- Notification quiet hours and alert threshold.

The questionnaire must detect inconsistent answers and ask the user to resolve them.

## 12. Technical stack

### Repository

```text
apps/
  web/             Next.js + TypeScript responsive UI
  api/             Python FastAPI application
  worker/          Python ingestion/analytics jobs
packages/
  schemas/         versioned JSON/OpenAPI schemas
  ui/              shared UI components
  provider-fixtures/
infra/
  cdk/             AWS CDK in TypeScript
docs/
```

### Production AWS baseline

- Amplify Hosting or S3/CloudFront for the web client.
- Cognito for authentication and MFA.
- API Gateway plus Lambda for the API.
- Lambda for short jobs; Fargate only for jobs that exceed Lambda limits.
- EventBridge Scheduler to start workflows.
- Step Functions for the research/recommendation workflow.
- SQS and dead-letter queues between provider ingestion and processing.
- Aurora PostgreSQL Serverless v2 for relational state.
- S3 for raw responses, filings, news bodies where licensed, generated briefs, and backtest snapshots.
- Bedrock through the Converse API for extraction, summaries, explanations, and grounded chat.
- Bedrock Guardrails plus application-level validation.
- Secrets Manager, KMS, WAF/rate limiting, CloudWatch, and CloudTrail.
- CDK for repeatable dev, staging, and production environments.

### Deliberately deferred infrastructure

- OpenSearch/Bedrock Knowledge Bases until PostgreSQL full-text search plus `pgvector` is measurably insufficient.
- Kubernetes.
- A streaming platform such as Kafka.
- Multi-region active/active deployment.

For five users, those add cost and operational burden without proving product value.

## 15. Security and AI-safety requirements

- Encrypt data in transit and at rest.
- Use least-privilege IAM and separate dev/staging/production accounts or environments.
- Require tenant isolation tests for every user-owned endpoint.
- Keep provider secrets in Secrets Manager and sensitive tokens encrypted with KMS.
- Never send names, account numbers, provider tokens, or unnecessary transaction descriptions to an LLM.
- Treat filings, articles, webpages, CSV text, and chat content as untrusted input.
- Retrieved text can provide facts but can never provide executable instructions.
- Do not give the explanatory model a trading tool.
- Log prompt template, model ID, evidence IDs, calculation version, output, and validation result.
- Implement user deletion, provider disconnect, retention policy, and audit-log policy.
- Rate-limit authentication, imports, chat, and provider refreshes.
- Show source/data freshness in the UI, not only in logs.

Bedrock Guardrails is defense-in-depth. AWS explicitly leaves application-level prompt-injection security to the customer, so schema validation, tool allowlists, ownership checks, and evidence verification are still required.

## 16. Regulatory product gates

This section is a design constraint, not legal advice.

U.S. regulators describe an investment adviser as a person or firm that, for compensation and as a business, provides advice or reports about securities. Automated advice is a delivery mechanism, not a separate exemption. Personalized recurring instructions such as "sell this holding and buy that ticker" are materially different from general education.

Before charging users, marketing personalized recommendations, or enabling execution:

1. Retain U.S. securities counsel.
2. Decide whether to register as an investment adviser, partner with an RIA, or restrict the product to impersonal analytics/education.
3. Confirm federal versus state registration requirements.
4. Create Form ADV/disclosures and a compliance program if the chosen model requires them.
5. Review conflicts, custody, discretionary authority, books/records, advertising, privacy, cybersecurity, and vendor oversight.
6. Approve market-data and brokerage licenses in writing.

A "not financial advice" footer does not change what the product actually does.

## 17. Testing and evaluation

### Software tests

- Unit tests for return, allocation, concentration, correlation, drawdown, tax-lot classification, and cash calculations.
- Provider contract tests using recorded fixtures.
- Split/dividend/corporate-action tests.
- Retry, duplicate-event, stale-data, and partial-provider-outage tests.
- Cross-user authorization tests.
- CSV formula-injection and malformed-import tests.
- Prompt-injection and evidence-spoofing tests.
- Mobile and accessibility tests.

### Recommendation-quality tests

- Point-in-time walk-forward backtests.
- Historical index membership to avoid survivorship bias.
- ALFRED or equivalent macro vintages to avoid revised-data leakage.
- Realistic fees, spread, slippage, turnover, and taxes where applicable.
- Baseline comparisons against buy-and-hold and simple diversified portfolios.
- Stability across repeated runs.
- Policy-violation rate.
- Citation coverage and factual-error rate.
- Alert precision: how many alerts were actually material?

Return is not the only success metric. The first quality bar is correct data, correct constraints, reproducible decisions, and useful explanations.

## 19. MVP acceptance criteria

The MVP is complete only when:

1. Five users have isolated accounts and cross-user tests pass.
2. Manual and CSV portfolios import with a visible validation report.
3. Portfolio calculations reconcile to fixtures within defined tolerances.
4. One shared market snapshot is reused for all users.
5. Only affected users are reevaluated after a security event.
6. Every decision card links to evidence and calculations with timestamps.
7. Stale, missing, contradictory, or unsupported data returns `INSUFFICIENT_DATA`.
8. Hard investment-policy constraints cannot be overridden by a prompt or model.
9. No code path can place a real order.
10. The full system works locally in mock mode and deploys to staging with CDK.
11. Important jobs and model calls have cost, latency, error, and freshness metrics.
12. A replay reconstructs exactly why a historical decision card was generated.

## 20. Cost-control rules

- Cache research by ticker and document, never by user.
- Use hashes to avoid re-summarizing unchanged documents.
- Run LLM analysis only after deterministic relevance filtering.
- Use small models for extraction/classification and stronger models only for final complex explanations.
- Cap evidence documents and tokens per task.
- Batch provider calls and use bulk endpoints.
- Reevaluate a user only after a material change.
- Do not add OpenSearch, streaming infrastructure, or native mobile until usage proves the need.
- Track cost per shared snapshot, ticker, decision card, and active user.
- Put hard daily budgets and alerts around data-provider and Bedrock usage.
